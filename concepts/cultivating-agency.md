# Cultivating Agency

## Definition

**Agency** is the disposition to treat the world as malleable and act on it — to make changes, ship things, reshape your own role — without waiting for permission or skill credentials. In the AI era, agency replaces skill as the rare, durable, compounding asset.

Coined (in this framing) by [Max Schoening](../entities/max-schoening.md), Head of Product at [Notion](../entities/notion.md):

> "Before, it was very easy to always say, 'Well, I will never be able to do this because insert skill issue.' We're realizing that even if you have the skills at your fingertips, the thing that matters is agency. I don't think agency is very evenly distributed in the world."

---

## Why It Matters

Two simultaneous shifts make agency the binding constraint:

1. **AI dissolves skill barriers.** Most "I can't do this because I'm not a coder / designer / writer" excuses no longer hold. The skill barrier was load-bearing for inaction; remove it and the question becomes: will you act?

2. **The first 10% of every project is free.** See [First 10% Is Free](../concepts/first-ten-percent-free.md). Prototypes, scaffolds, directional sketches — all free. The differentiator collapses to whether you *start*.

When skills are commoditized and starting is free, the rare thing is the person who *decides and ships*. That person captures disproportionate value. That person is also rare — Schoening: *"I don't think agency is very evenly distributed in the world."*

---

## The Deep Source: "People No Smarter Than You"

Schoening attributes his own agency to a Steve Jobs quote that hits him repeatedly:

> "One day you wake up and you realize the world is made up by people no smarter than you."

Lenny independently surfaces hearing the same insight from Brian Chesky at Airbnb. The realization: the things around you — products, processes, organizations, even physical infrastructure — are made by humans, not delivered by physics. They can be changed. Most people don't change them because they assume someone smarter already had the right reasons.

Schoening's closing ask to listeners makes this concrete: *"Go for a walk in whatever city you're in. Carefully look at how everything around you is made up by people that are no smarter than you and realize that probably in the span of six to nine months, you can, for most things around you, figure out how to make it from scratch — and therefore you have much more agency than you think."*

---

## How to Cultivate It

Schoening's prescription is **make things**, not navigate org politics:

> "When a lot of people hear agency, they think of themselves as in this big machine, and they're like, 'Oh, okay, I'm going to circumvent my terrible boss or manager so that I get X, Y, and Z.' It's like — no, no, just start by making things. And usually when you get better at making things, at some point people pay attention, and it just really awakens you to the idea that you can just change things."

The mechanism:
1. **Tinker.** Cook a meal. Build a chair. Fix a tool. Make a side project. The toolmaking is the point.
2. **Through making, build the felt sense that things are malleable.** This is the awakening — not an intellectual conviction but an embodied one.
3. **Get on the treadmill.** Making is generative; one act of making produces the next.
4. **People notice.** Recognition follows from output, not from positioning.

Negative form: *"I'm going to circumvent the system"* is **not** agency in Schoening's sense. It's still working *within* the org as the determinant. Real agency is indifferent to the org — it changes the role, the product, the world.

## Examples (Notion-internal)

**Brian Levin/Lovin (designer)**: Already blurs design and engineering. Also the #1 recruiter on the team because "this is what the org needs." Doesn't wait for a recruiting team to flag the gap.

**Eric Lou (PM)**: Asked Schoening, *"If you started a startup, would you hire me in the first 10?"* Schoening said no, didn't need a PM. Lou: *"Okay, I'll work on the skills so you'd hire me in the first five."* Over the following months, he dropped PRDs in favor of Figma, then dropped Figma in favor of building prototypes himself. Reinvented the role around the change he wanted to see.

**Schoening himself**: *"Do you drive Notion like it's stolen?"* Notion's PMs are not the founders — they joined after PMF — but the operating norm is to act like founders anyway.

## Relationship to Adjacent Concepts

- **[Builder vs. Information Mover](../concepts/builder-vs-information-mover.md)** — Builders are the archetype produced by agency. Information movers are the archetype produced by its absence. Nikhyl Singhal's split describes *what kind of PM you become*; cultivating agency describes *the underlying disposition that produces that PM.*
- **[Don't Be Fungible](../concepts/dont-be-fungible.md)** — non-fungible combinations are the *strategic output* of high-agency career-building. Without agency, you can build a great résumé and still be fungible; with agency, you reshape your own combination.
- **[Shadow Superpower](../concepts/shadow-superpower.md)** — high-skill / low-agency operators are the ones most resistant to the AI transition. Skill mastery becomes a substitute for action.
- **[First 10% Is Free](../concepts/first-ten-percent-free.md)** — the structural condition that makes agency newly binding. When starting is free, willingness to start is the constraint.
- **[Malleable Software](../concepts/malleable-software.md)** — the product-level expression of the same disposition. You want users with agency *over* their software. Agency at work + malleable software = the same idea at different layers.
- **[Ant vs. Break-in (Alarm Calibration)](../concepts/ant-vs-break-in.md)** — the *physiological* complement. Schoening's agency thesis lives at the psychological layer; [Matthew LaBosco](../entities/matthew-labosco.md) extends the same idea into the nervous-system register: **"should" is the curse word of the nervous system** — *"If you have to, should, or need to — you've lost your agency. Cortisol stays high into the night."* A day full of resentment-laced obligation registers as ongoing threat, fragments sleep, and degrades next-day judgment. Low psychological agency *and* low physiological calibration produce the same downstream effect (degraded action capacity), and they reinforce each other: a body running an unregulated threat response will reach for the lowest-agency option in every decision because high-agency action requires reserves. The pair: psychological agency requires a calibrated body; a calibrated body requires psychological agency over which obligations are real. See [LaBosco — 9 Ways to Drop Cortisol](../essays/labosco-cortisol-sleep-9-fixes.md).
- **[Founder Advantage Stack](../concepts/founder-advantage-stack.md)** — Gurley's determination dimension is agency sustained against resistance. Agency is broader (the disposition to act and reshape); founder determination is narrower (continued commitment to a particular problem). The distinction prevents confusing stubborn attachment to a solution with high agency.
- **[Influence vs. Concern](../concepts/influence-vs-concern.md)** — a practical attention filter for agency. It prevents concern about uncontrollable outcomes from impersonating action and converts attention into response choices.
- **[Micro-Discipline](../concepts/micro-discipline.md)** — the daily repetition layer beneath agency. Schoening emphasizes making things and reshaping reality; Stumpf supplies the smaller behavioral unit that builds confidence in one's own follow-through.

## PM Application

For a Senior Director of PM at Microsoft:

1. **Add an explicit agency signal to PM hiring rubrics.** Existing rubrics emphasize impact, scope, and influence — all gameable by a skilled, low-agency operator working through hierarchy. Add: *"Show me something you shipped without being asked. What did you have to push past?"* If the answer is "I aligned stakeholders" — information movement. If it's "I built this myself" — agency. Calibrate accordingly.

2. **Stress-test whether your culture punishes agency.** The Eric Lou story would get many Microsoft PMs in trouble: rewriting your role, dropping deliverables, prototyping ahead of consensus. If the rhetoric is "drive it like it's stolen" but the behavior is "submit a proposal first," the rhetoric is hollow. Specific test: identify and *publicly reward* one role-reinvention story per quarter on your team.

3. **Manager filter.** PM managers were often promoted for synthesis and coordination — the skills of high-information-movement environments. They tend to select for their own archetype in hiring and performance. Check whether your manager layer has agency themselves before relying on them to develop it in others. See [Shadow Superpower](../concepts/shadow-superpower.md).

4. **Career path reform.** The traditional ladder rewards graduating *away* from making (IC → lead → manager → director through influence and synthesis). The AI-era ladder needs to reward *returning* to making at every level. Schoening himself wishes he could replace 80% of his job with agent loops. Senior leaders who still build are the new prestige model.

5. **Don't conflate agency with insubordination.** Schoening explicitly rejects the "circumvent your manager" frame. Agency is forward-facing (make things, change reality), not adversarial (work around the org). The Microsoft cultural risk is that agency gets coded as a political move. It isn't. It's a creative move.

6. **Build environments that awaken agency.** Schoening: making is the on-ramp; people don't intellectualize their way into agency. Microsoft analogs: internal hackathons that actually ship to production, agent-builder access for non-engineers, "build it yourself in a weekend" customs that get celebrated. The org wants people on the making treadmill.
