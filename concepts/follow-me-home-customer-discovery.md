# Follow Me Home Customer Discovery

## Definition

An observation-and-voting method for product discovery: put real users in front of decision-makers, ask them to identify concrete fixes and desired capabilities, then use forced voting to reveal the small number of priorities that matter most.

## Why It Matters

Traditional feedback often produces a long, flat list of requests detached from emotion and context. Follow Me Home adds three missing signals: what users experience firsthand, how strongly they feel it, and what they prioritize when forced to trade off.

## Method

1. Bring real users into the room; managers observe rather than lead.
2. Ask each user for three things to fix.
3. Ask for three things they wish the product had.
4. Give each user five votes to stack or distribute.
5. Look for convergence instead of treating every request equally.
6. Let managers carry the users’ frustration and enthusiasm into roadmap decisions.

## Example

Intuit founder Scott Cook taught McNeill this practice. McNeill later ran it with Lyft drivers, and the resulting priorities shaped approximately the following year’s roadmap.

## PM Application

Use this as a complement to analytics, surveys, and interviews—not as a replacement for them. For Copilot or family-assistant discovery, observe users performing real work, ask what should be fixed and what they wish existed, then force prioritization. The product opportunity is often visible in the mismatch between what users say they want and where they actually lose time, confidence, or momentum.

## Family Assistant Adaptation

Observe a real chaotic morning with Kripal and Pari. Ask each:

- What are the three things Sally should fix?
- What are the three things you wish Sally could do?

Give each person five votes, then use the convergence as input to the next capability wedge.
