# Influence vs. Concern

## Definition

A written attention-allocation exercise shared by [Andy Stumpf](../entities/andy-stumpf.md): externalize everything occupying the mind, separate broad concerns from what one can influence directly, and convert the influence side into specific choices about attention, speech, planning, time, and response.

The framework does not claim control over outcomes. It distinguishes **impactable response** from **unproductive mental occupation**.

## Why It Matters

Concern feels like work because it consumes cognitive and physiological resources. But repeated monitoring of an uncontrollable issue rarely changes it. Without an explicit sorting mechanism, leaders can spend their highest-quality attention on news, rumors, executive mood, competitor narratives, and organizational drama while postponing the few actions available to them.

Writing makes the imbalance visible. The concern side is typically large; the influence side is small. That asymmetry is clarifying rather than discouraging because the short list can be scheduled and completed.

## The Practice

1. Take a blank page and draw a vertical line.
2. Label the left side **Concern** and the right side **Influence**.
3. List every issue occupying attention without filtering.
4. For each issue, ask: “What can I directly change today—my attention, preparation, words, time, request, decision, or response?”
5. Put those actions under Influence. Do not move the desired outcome itself.
6. Choose one to three actions and put them on the calendar.
7. When the concern returns, use the page to distinguish new information from another loop through the same worry.

**Cadence:** Run weekly during volatile periods, or whenever a thought repeatedly interrupts sleep or focused work. Stumpf reports using it less frequently once the distinction became habitual; Huberman reports a weekly cadence.

## Examples

| Concern | Influence |
|---|---|
| Reorganization outcome | Document team value, protect critical work, clarify contingency plans |
| Competitor model launch | Test it on three real workflows; revise one product assumption |
| Executive reaction | Improve the decision narrative; request a direct conversation |
| Customer dissatisfaction | Call the customer; reproduce the failure; assign an owner |
| Industry anxiety about AI | Reserve hands-on learning time; build one capability this month |

## PM Application

For a Senior Director of PM at Microsoft:

- Begin weekly planning with the two-column page before opening Teams or email. This prevents inbound salience from deciding the agenda.
- Use it during reorganizations to separate scenario planning from rumor consumption.
- Apply it to strategy reviews: if an identified risk has no influence-side action, decide whether it belongs in monitoring, contingency planning, or nowhere on the active agenda.
- Design Copilot workflows that turn concern statements into bounded influence options while preserving uncertainty. The product should not promise control it cannot provide.
- Pair it with [Ant vs. Break-in](../concepts/ant-vs-break-in.md): the latter calibrates urgency; this framework calibrates agency.
