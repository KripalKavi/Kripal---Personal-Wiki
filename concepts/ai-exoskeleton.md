# AI Exoskeleton

## Definition

An **AI exoskeleton** is a layer of AI context, decision support, and execution capability wrapped around each worker so they can act with more speed and leverage. The organizational goal is not to automate away all people; it is to reduce the need for management layers whose primary function is moving information.

## Why It Matters

Most organizations scale by adding coordination: managers manage managers, meetings distribute context, and decisions wait for people who possess fragments of the relevant information. An AI exoskeleton can widen access to context and make the point of work more capable, collapsing some of that hierarchy.

The risk is building an artifact-generation layer that increases review burden. The exoskeleton only works if it connects workers to raw facts, customer reality, permissions, and clear decision rights.

## Examples

- Nejatian describes Opendoor giving every employee an AI layer to support fast, low-cost decisions around operationally complex physical assets.
- A Microsoft PM exoskeleton would connect product telemetry, customer evidence, support signals, experiment history, engineering state, and decision records in the flow of work.
- A company-wide agent can provide shared context, while specialized agents handle recurring analysis. The accountable human still owns the choice.

## PM Application

1. Map where managers primarily move information, then make that context directly available to the people doing the work.
2. Measure decision latency, rework, escalation volume, and ownership—not just generated output or agent calls.
3. Build reality contact into the system: raw data, customer conversations, frontline artifacts, and product usage should be first-class inputs.
4. Preserve explicit human decision rights for consequential choices; AI should expand context and execution capacity, not launder accountability.
