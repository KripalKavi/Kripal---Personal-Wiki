# Founder Advantage Stack

## Definition

A synthesis of [Bill Gurley's](../entities/bill-gurley.md) founder traits:

1. **Product instinct** identifies what should exist and what feels wrong before consensus forms.
2. **Storytelling** recruits employees, capital, customers, executives, and partners into that future.
3. **Determination** sustains the mission when evidence is incomplete and obstacles compound.

The three are multiplicative rather than interchangeable. Product instinct without storytelling stays under-resourced. Storytelling without product instinct mobilizes people around the wrong thing. Both without determination collapse during the inevitable difficult middle.

## Why It Matters

Large-company leadership systems often overweight the most legible component—storytelling—because it is easiest to observe in reviews. Gurley's model exposes the resulting false positive: an executive can communicate brilliantly without possessing unusual product judgment or costly commitment to the problem.

The framework also challenges the assumption that every component is equally coachable. Gurley regards product-first instinct as exceptionally hard to retrofit, suggesting that organizations should select strongly for it and develop the communication system around it.

## Examples

- A founder notices an emerging workflow before research quantifies it (**instinct**), explains a credible future that attracts a small exceptional team (**storytelling**), and keeps iterating after the first implementation fails (**determination**).
- A senior PM produces a persuasive strategy deck but has not used the competing products and delegates prototype judgment. The stack is storytelling-heavy and product-light.
- A craft-obsessed builder repeatedly ships but cannot recruit partners or explain why the work matters. The stack is product-heavy but resource-constrained.

## Assessment Prompts

- **Product instinct:** What did you believe about a product before the dashboard or customer consensus supported you? What artifact did you make to test it?
- **Storytelling:** Whose behavior or commitment changed because of your narrative? What did they do afterward?
- **Determination:** What costly work continued before approval, funding, or recognition arrived? Which tactics did you abandon while preserving the mission?
- **Integration:** Describe a case where user evidence changed the story and the implementation, but not the underlying problem you were determined to solve.

## PM Application

For a Senior Director of PM at Microsoft:

- Score product leadership candidates separately on all three dimensions. Do not allow executive communication to average away weak product evidence.
- Put product-instinct evidence first in hiring loops: teardowns, prototypes, judgment calls, and original observations. Communication can then be evaluated on whether it mobilizes resources around sound judgment.
- In incubation reviews, distinguish mission determination from solution attachment. Reward leaders who persist on the problem while killing weak implementations.
- Build complementary founding teams when no individual has the full stack—but keep product decision rights with genuine product-first leadership.
- Connect the stack to [Obviously Good](../concepts/obviously-good.md): instinct recognizes the quality bar; storytelling makes it legible; determination closes the difficult final gap.
