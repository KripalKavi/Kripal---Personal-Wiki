# Stress-Response Calibration

## Definition

Match the response to the controllability of the stressor: apply effort and agency when action can change the outcome; accept, release, and redirect attention when the situation is an immovable wall.

## Why it matters

Many high performers treat every stressor as a command to push harder. Epel’s model identifies that as a category error: challenge can build resilience, but rumination and futile struggle keep the alarm active and consume scarce cognitive and physiological resources.

## Examples

- **Product problem:** assign an owner and next experiment when evidence can change the outcome.
- **Organizational constraint:** stop replaying an executive decision that cannot be changed; redirect to the decisions still open.
- **Uncertainty:** use a slower breath and receptive posture before trying to forecast every branch.
- **Team stress:** replace vague “be resilient” language with a specific stress shield and a recovery boundary.

## PM Application

Add a controllability check to planning and incident reviews: “What can we change, influence, or only accept?” Require each category to produce a different operating action. This reduces performative urgency, protects attention, and makes leadership behavior more legible to the team.
