# Big Bets Growth Strategy

---

## Definition

A growth resource allocation philosophy where 70% of capacity (time, headcount, engineering) is directed at major, transformational bets and only 30% at incremental experiments. This is a deliberate inversion of most traditional growth team playbooks, where the ratio is reversed.

Coined (or at least described publicly) by Amol Avasare at [Anthropic](../entities/anthropic.md) to describe their approach to scaling Claude's growth.

---

## Why It Matters

**The standard growth model**: Heavy A/B testing, constant funnel optimization, incremental conversion improvements. The compounding logic is: small improvements stack up over time.

**Anthropic's counter-argument**: In a fast-moving market (particularly AI), the opportunity cost of incremental optimization is extremely high because:
- The landscape shifts faster than incremental improvements can compound
- Large bets can change the trajectory entirely; incremental bets can only optimize within it
- When you're already growing fast, the primary constraint is not conversion rate — it's the scale of the opportunity you're unlocking
- The most important competitive moves are not discoverable through A/B testing

---

## The Anthropic Model

Amol Avasare describes the specific ratio as a conscious inversion:

| Allocation | Typical growth team | Anthropic |
|------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Major bets | 30% | **70%** |
| Incremental experiments | 70% | **30%** |

Major bets at Anthropic include:
- Building CASH (AI-automated experimentation system — itself a bet that automation unlocks more value than manual optimization)
- Redesigning the full activation journey — not just optimizing one step in it
- Investing in AI coding as the primary growth vector (a category bet, not a funnel bet)

---

## When This Applies

This philosophy is most defensible when:
1. **Fast-moving market**: Early AI market positioning matters more than marginal efficiency gains
2. **Product still being defined**: Discovery value of large bets is high when you don't yet know the full shape of the opportunity
3. **High directional confidence**: The large bets are not random moonshots — they're grounded in strong conviction about what matters
4. **High engineering leverage**: Sufficient capacity to run multiple major bets in parallel

It is less defensible when:
- The market is mature and the product is well-defined
- The team lacks the conviction or data to make large directional bets
- Engineering capacity is constrained (incremental work is cheaper to execute)

---

## PM Application

For a Senior Director of PM at Microsoft:

1. **Audit current allocation**: What percentage of PM and engineering effort is genuinely transformational vs. incremental optimization? Most large-company teams default to incremental because it generates metrics faster and is easier to justify in planning cycles.

2. **Name the big bets explicitly**: For a big-bet philosophy to work, the bets must be explicitly defined and protected — not just "we'll do more important work." Define: what are the 2-3 transformational bets this team owns, and how much capacity is explicitly ring-fenced for them?

3. **Protect bets from metric pressure**: Incremental work generates visible metrics quickly; transformational bets take longer to show results. Without explicit protection, teams will unconsciously shift toward the work that looks good in weekly reviews. Leadership must actively protect the big-bet time.

4. **Apply at the right scope**: 70/30 is a team-level philosophy, not a company-level mandate. A team that owns a mature, stable product feature may be in a different position than a team working on a new AI use case. Apply the principle where the market dynamics warrant it.

5. **Pair with conviction discipline**: Big bets only work when you're right about the direction. Requires strong product intuition, user insight, and competitive awareness — or expensive thrashing. The ratio is not the hard part; the quality of the bets is.
