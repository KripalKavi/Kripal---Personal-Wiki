# P0 / Two-Way Door Decisioning

## Definition

Make the current priority explicit, classify decisions by reversibility, and move quickly on choices that do not threaten the P0. Reserve deep deliberation and escalation for genuinely one-way decisions or choices that directly affect the bottleneck.

## Why It Matters

Organizations confuse importance with visibility. This framework filters out socially rewarded distractions while reducing the fear that makes ordinary decisions feel irreversible. Its discipline is not “move fast” in the abstract; it is continuous re-ranking against a concrete priority.

## Examples

- Re-rank a daily list until the single most important item is obvious and protected.
- If a proposal is irrelevant to the P0, decide and move rather than polishing the decision.
- If it distracts from the P0, say no even when external stakeholders reward the meeting or activity.
- Treat a reversible product experiment as a step on the staircase, not a life-or-death strategic bet.

## PM Application

1. Publish one P0 per team or planning horizon.
2. Label roadmap and operating decisions as one-way or two-way doors.
3. Measure decision latency for reversible choices and reduce unnecessary approval layers.
4. Review whether executive anxiety is being mistaken for company priority.
5. Re-rank priorities whenever model capability, customer evidence, or business constraints materially change.
