# Near-Term Model Horizon

## Definition

The near-term model horizon is the idea that AI products should be built for where the model will likely be in the next few months, not where it is today and not where speculative long-range forecasts say it might be in a year.

## Why It Matters

Model capability moves too quickly for either extreme to work:

- Build only for today's model and you over-scaffold around weaknesses that will soon disappear.
- Build for a far-off fantasy model and you ship something that never maps to reality.

The practical path is to build on a short horizon, keep the structure thin, and remove the harness as the model improves.

## Examples

- A PM designs a feature with just enough guardrails for this quarter's model, then plans to delete them when quality rises.
- A team reviews model capability every release window before committing to a deeper product abstraction.

## PM Application

For Microsoft product teams:

1. Tie roadmap reviews to a short model-horizon checklist.
2. Separate durable product value from temporary scaffolding.
3. Revisit assumptions frequently so the team does not keep paying for dead weight.
4. Avoid long-range architecture bets that depend on capabilities the model does not yet have.
