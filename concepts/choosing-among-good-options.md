# Choosing Among Good Options

## Definition

The strategic problem is often not finding a correct answer. It is selecting the best path from several valid options when each has different time horizons, risks, and feedback loops.

## Why It Matters

Organizations systematically favor options with visible short-term feedback: near-term revenue, familiar implementations, or metrics that move within the quarter. That can make the locally measurable choice look more rational than a deeper rebuild or market shift whose payoff arrives later.

The absence of immediate feedback is not evidence that a path is wrong. It is a signal that judgment, historical pattern recognition, and explicit long-term reasoning matter more. Metrics are useful proxies, but once a proxy becomes the objective, teams optimize the measurement instead of the system.

## Examples

- Shopify could have continued optimizing a profitable snowboard store; the more valuable path was turning the underlying capability into a broader commerce platform.
- An AI-generated pull request can be technically plausible while still being a poor organizational choice if review and correction costs exceed its value.
- A platform rebuild may be locally painful and invisible in quarterly dashboards while creating much larger strategic options later.

## PM Application

In strategy reviews, require teams to:

1. List the valid options rather than present one favored answer.
2. Name which option has the fastest feedback and which has the largest long-term option value.
3. Separate the mission metric from the proxy metrics that can be gamed.
4. State what evidence would arrive late, what cannot be measured directly, and who owns the judgment until then.
5. Include the cost of review, verification, and organizational complexity—not just the amount of output produced.

For Microsoft AI products, this is a defense against optimizing Copilot for visible interaction volume while degrading trust, user capability, or the coherence of the broader workflow.

Nejatian adds the operating counterpart: once a choice is made, truth-telling and direct contact with the terrain are what reveal whether the chosen path is working. Delaying a hard decision to gather endless facts usually increases risk; make the decision, inspect reality, and correct quickly.
