# Micro-Discipline

## Definition

The practice of choosing the slightly harder **useful** option more often in small, ordinary moments—especially when the choice is invisible to others. The aim is not maximum difficulty. It is repeated completion of the action one already knows will reduce future friction or align behavior with intent.

## Why It Matters

People often seek a dramatic protocol because it creates a clean identity: hard workout, heroic deadline, public challenge. Stumpf relocates discipline to unremarkable choices such as finishing a loop, preparing ahead, or doing a task correctly at first contact.

These choices compound in two ways:

1. **Operationally:** completed loops do not return later as emergencies, coordination debt, or cleanup.
2. **Psychologically:** repeated follow-through creates evidence that discomfort can be tolerated and commitments to oneself are credible.

The “slightly” constraint matters. Seeking the hardest option can become performative, unsafe, or counterproductive. The practice is a directional bias, not self-punishment or perfectionism.

## Practice

1. **Select a daily anchor:** Make the bed, drink water before coffee, prepare tomorrow's first meal, or close the work surface at day's end.
2. **Name the fork:** When tempted to defer, state the two options plainly: “close the loop now” versus “transfer it to later.”
3. **Choose slightly harder, not maximally hard:** Prefer the smallest action that completes or materially advances the loop.
4. **Target invisible behavior:** Do not post, score, or turn every act into identity theater.
5. **Reset without drama:** A missed action is the next repetition opportunity, not evidence of failure.
6. **Review weekly:** Identify one recurring shortcut whose deferred cost exceeds its apparent convenience.

## Examples

- Replace an empty item rather than leaving it for the next person.
- Record a product decision while its reasoning is fresh rather than reconstructing it after disagreement returns.
- Fix a flaky evaluation before adding another scenario that depends on it.
- Give precise feedback now rather than allowing ambiguity to compound through a review cycle.
- Prepare a real breakfast the night before rather than relying on morning willpower.

## PM Application

For Microsoft product leadership:

- Track **open-loop age**: how long known decisions, bugs, ownership gaps, and customer follow-ups remain unresolved. This is a better micro-discipline metric than meeting volume.
- End staff meetings by closing one small recurring coordination defect, not merely assigning more work.
- Apply “do it right at first contact” to AI evals, telemetry definitions, and launch documentation—areas where shortcuts become organizational memory defects.
- Reward quiet prevention alongside visible rescues. Otherwise the culture selects for firefighting rather than discipline.
- Pair micro-discipline with [Obviously Good](../concepts/obviously-good.md): quality is often the accumulated result of hundreds of small, correct decisions that never become launch slides.
