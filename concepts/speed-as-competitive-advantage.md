# Speed as Competitive Advantage

## Definition

Speed is not merely working harder; it is reducing the time between observing a problem, deciding, acting, receiving feedback, and reallocating resources.

## Why It Matters

Decision speed, response speed, cash speed, and learning speed compound when competitors move more slowly. The advantage comes from shorter loops, not from sustaining maximum intensity at all times.

## Examples

- A factory-floor observation led to an overnight jig for Model X door assembly.
- Fast escalation and short written communication reduce the cost of bad news.
- The right cadence alternates deliberate problem framing with decisive execution.

## PM Application

Measure speed as a system property: time to detect, decide, ship, learn, and correct. Protect teams from performative urgency by distinguishing the framing phase from the execution phase; once the path is clear, remove avoidable waiting.
