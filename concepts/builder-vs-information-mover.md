## Definition

The two archetypes of PM in the AI era, as framed by Nikhyl Singhal:

**Builder**: Got into product because they love to build. Technical instinct. Direct customer connection. Comfortable exercising judgment without consensus. The builders are in high demand right now — record comp, most open PM roles in 3+ years, expanding career optionality (founder, CEO, CHRO, cross-function leadership).

**Information Mover**: Got into product for the communication, facilitation, and synthesis skills. Core job is translating information between levels of the org, managing alignment, framing for leadership. Valuable in large-org ZIRP-era structures where coordination was the bottleneck. Becoming obsolete as AI handles the information layer.

Singhal estimates roughly **50% of PMs** are primarily information movers. That half faces structural displacement.

## Why It Matters

The split determines who thrives vs. who becomes redundant. Information movement — synthesizing data, reframing for leadership, writing status reports, running standups to maintain alignment — is precisely what AI automates best. Building and judgment are what AI amplifies but cannot replace.

This is **not a seniority split**. Information movers exist at every level. A VP who primarily facilitates and synthesizes is an information mover. An IC who builds prototypes and exercises direct product judgment is a builder. Level doesn't determine archetype; relationship to the work does.

The transition also **inverts the career development model**: PMs are traditionally trained to *stop* building as they advance ("stop working on the car, work on the factory"). Now the industry is asking the most senior people to *return* to building. This is disorienting for people who spent years climbing away from it.

[Bill Gurley](../entities/bill-gurley.md) adds a selection warning: product instinct appears exceptionally difficult to retrofit into someone who is not product-first. That makes the builder/information-mover distinction relevant not only to development but to hiring and succession. See [Founder Advantage Stack](../concepts/founder-advantage-stack.md) and [Bedrock and Bleeding Edge](../concepts/bedrock-and-bleeding-edge.md).

## Information Mover behaviors (increasingly at risk)

- Rewriting team updates to "frame them correctly" for leadership
- Running weekly syncs that exist primarily to keep stakeholders aligned
- Translating between engineering and business in documents and meetings
- Managing up by controlling information flow and narrative
- Career defined by "influencing without authority" through communication and synthesis

## Builder behaviors (increasingly valued)

- Prototyping directly to test assumptions before bringing in engineering
- Writing scripts or AI workflows to automate their own operational overhead
- Doing user research that generates novel insight rather than reporting aggregated findings
- Exercising judgment on which of 100 AI-generated changes to ship vs. discard
- Building internal tools that eliminate manual processes from the team's workflow

## PM Application

**For a Senior Director running a large PM org:**

1. **Team audit — be honest about archetypes**: Map your PMs to the spectrum based on how their best days actually look, not their job description or level. Many "senior" PMs may be senior information movers. This is uncomfortable but necessary.

2. **Hiring recalibration**: Screen for building instinct, not logo or title. Ask candidates what they've built recently — a side project, an internal tool, a prototype, anything. "I don't code" is no longer an adequate answer for a PM at any level. Judgment on what to build requires hands-on familiarity with how it gets built.

3. **Career path reorientation**: The old path (IC → lead → manager → director through stakeholder influence and information synthesis) needs a new track where hands-on building is an accelerator at every level — not something you "graduate from" as you get more senior.

4. **Manager protection risk**: Many PM managers were promoted for synthesis and coordination skills. They may inadvertently select for their own archetype in hiring and performance reviews. Check your manager layer against the same framework.

5. **Information movement as a transition target**: Work that used to require a senior PM to "translate and frame" for leadership can increasingly be done by an agent or automated workflow. Explicitly identifying these tasks and reassigning them to tooling frees your PMs to do judgment work — and also signals clearly what the new job looks like.

## Independent corroboration: Shipper's "PMs thrive"

[Dan Shipper](../entities/dan-shipper.md) (Every) makes the same claim from a different angle on Lenny's, 2026-05-24: PMs will *thrive* in the AI era because the bottleneck moves to **directing agent behavior and maintaining user-value alignment** — exactly the judgment-and-building work that defines the builder PM. Shipper's framing reinforces the bifurcation: agents amplify the part of the job that requires direction and judgment, while automating the information-movement layer. See [The AI Paradox](../podcasts/lenny-dan-shipper-ai-paradox.md) and [Automation Is a Lie](../concepts/automation-is-a-lie.md).

This is independent triangulation from two senior practitioners (Singhal at Skip/ex-Meta/Google; Shipper at Every) running different experiments. Both arrive at: the builder PM thrives; the information-mover PM does not. That convergence is the load-bearing signal — not either one alone.
