# How to Stay Current, Get Hired, and Last in AI

## Metadata

- **Show:** The Skip Podcast
- **Host:** Nikhyl Singhal
- **Guests:** Dana Ingraham, Hemal Shah, and Jiaona “JZ” Zhang
- **Published:** 2026-11-12
- **Duration:** approximately 54 minutes
- **Official episode:** [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/episode/3saSjBbL5ix0ZMmrAXMoKv)
- **Guest moves discussed:** Dana joined Harvey after seven years at Airbnb; Hemal moved from OpenAI to Google Cloud; JZ became CPO of Laurel after Webflow.

## Summary

Nikhyl Singhal talks with three product leaders who recently moved into AI roles about staying current, getting hired, and sustaining demanding careers while raising young families. They reject the idea that candidates must be “AI-pilled” or consume every new model release; the durable advantage is a combination of domain history, technical understanding, customer empathy, curiosity, adaptability, and proximity to real work.

## Key Takeaways

- **You do not need to be AI-pilled to get an AI job.** Dana’s counterpoint is that strong product judgment, customer understanding, and the ability to learn can matter more than performative tool enthusiasm. Candidates should show how they use AI to improve their work, not collect AI vocabulary.
- **The durable PM profile is historian + technologist + anthropologist.** Hemal’s three-part model: understand how the field got here, understand what the technology can and cannot do, and understand people deeply enough to see behavior and unmet needs.
- **Curiosity and adaptation beat exhaustive consumption.** No one can follow every paper, model, launch, and opinion. Stay current through the work itself and through “people curation”: maintain a small, high-signal network of practitioners whose judgment you trust.
- **Choose the company’s change capacity, not its valuation.** Hemal’s company screen is data, distribution, people, and willingness to change. A prestigious or highly valued company without an edge or appetite for reinvention may be a poor AI career platform.
- **The job is often the best curriculum.** A role close to customers, deployment, operations, or the frontier can teach more in two months than passive study over a year.
- **Do not wait for the perfect PM title.** JZ recommends forward-deployed and AI-operations roles as high-leverage entry points. They put candidates directly in the loop between users, product, implementation, and model capability.
- **Unique data is a moat only when connected to workflow and distribution.** JZ’s Laurel example reinforces that proprietary data becomes valuable when the product earns the right to generate and use it inside a repeated workflow.
- **Sustainable ambition requires ruthless prioritization.** The useful question is: “What is one thing I would regret not doing with my time right now?” The guests connect endurance less to perfect time management than to protecting sources of joy and meaning.

## How to Prepare for an AI Role

The guests’ examples imply a practical preparation plan:

1. **Build a working relationship with the tools.** Use current AI tools on real work or a small personal project; learn where they are useful, unreliable, and still surprisingly weak.
2. **Keep your domain advantage.** Map the customer workflow, economics, constraints, and history of the problem you want to work on. AI fluency without a problem domain is shallow signal.
3. **Develop technical judgment, not just tool familiarity.** Be able to discuss capability boundaries, data quality, evaluation, latency/cost, human review, and what should not be automated.
4. **Create evidence of shipping.** A prototype, customer workflow improvement, forward-deployed implementation, evaluation harness, or internal tool is stronger than a list of courses or model names.
5. **Curate your information network.** Follow a few builders and users with direct experience; compare their observations rather than trying to read everything.
6. **Target the learning surface.** Ask which role will put you closest to customers, real deployments, proprietary data, and fast feedback. Optimize for the move after the next move, not just title or compensation.

## PM Relevance

For Microsoft, this is a useful hiring and talent-development lens. Evaluate AI PM candidates on the combination of historical context, technical judgment, anthropology of the user, and evidence of learning through real work. Create internal “forward-deployed” opportunities—customer pilots, deployment sprints, eval operations, or workflow automation—so existing PMs can acquire AI fluency without needing a title change first. For career decisions, assess whether a team has data, distribution, talent density, and the willingness to change; brand and valuation are weaker signals than the quality of the learning loop.
