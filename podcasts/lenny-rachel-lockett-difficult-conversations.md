## Key Takeaways

### Three Levels of Listening

Most leaders think they're listening but are operating at Level 1 or 2:

- **Level 1 — Internal listening**: You're present physically but your attention is on your own thoughts, preparing your response, or distracted. "People don't listen; they just wait for their turn to speak."
- **Level 2 — Focused listening**: You're tracking the words. You understand what the person says. This is where most "good listeners" operate.
- **Level 3 — Global listening**: You attend to what's *beneath* the words — tone, body language, emotional subtext, what isn't being said. You pick up on the gap between the words and the actual experience. This is where real coaching happens.

The skill gap between Level 2 and Level 3 is enormous. Level 3 is what distinguishes a coach from a smart person who asks good questions. You can only access it when you're not thinking about what to say next.

---

### Coaching vs. Directing — The Fundamental Management Distinction

- Two modes: directing (tell people what to do) vs. coaching (guide people to their own solution)
- Most managers over-index on directing because it's faster in the moment — but it stunts the team
- The right question: is this person capable of reaching the right answer with guidance? If yes, coach. If there's a genuine blocker, a time constraint, or the stakes are too high — direct.
- Failing to distinguish these modes is the defining failure of high-performing ICs who become managers: they have strong views and the knowledge to back them, so they default to telling

### GROW Framework — Live Demonstration

See [GROW Coaching Framework](../concepts/grow-coaching-framework.md) for full detail. The episode includes a live coaching session with Lenny about his newsletter overwhelm / work-life balance, walking through each stage in real time:

- **Goal**: What does Lenny want? Not surface ("less stress") but the actual desired state
- **Reality**: Lenny describes the Indiana Jones boulder — the newsletter feels like a giant boulder chasing him and he can't stop running
- **Options**: What could he change? They explore concrete options: hire help, cut scope, reframe the relationship to the work
- **Way Forward**: Specific commitment — not "I'll think about it" but a real next action

The power of the demo: watching someone shift from anxiety-framing to agency-framing in real time is the clearest illustration of why GROW works.

### Nonviolent Communication (NVC) for Conflict

See [Nonviolent Communication](../concepts/nonviolent-communication.md) for full framework. Marshall Rosenberg's four-step model for difficult conversations:

1. **Observations**: Factual, photographable — what happened, stripped of interpretation
2. **Feelings**: Actual emotions, not "I feel *that* you..." (which is a judgment disguised as a feeling)
3. **Needs**: Universal human needs — connection, recognition, fairness — without assigning blame
4. **Request**: A small, achievable olive branch. Not a demand. Specific, doable now.

"Stay on your side of the net" — from Stanford's Touchy Feely course (Carol Robbins). Your job is to own your observations, feelings, and needs — not to characterize the other person's intentions or behavior. The moment you cross the net (characterize them) you trigger defensiveness and the conversation closes.

Goal of conflict: **mutual understanding**, not convincing. Most people approach conflict trying to win; NVC reframes conflict as two people trying to understand what's at stake for each other.

Conflict reframe: *"When we are in conflict, something important is at stake."* Lean into it as a signal, not something to manage away.

### The Enthusiastic Rehire Question

From Stripe's talent practice. The question: **"Would I enthusiastically rehire this person for the same role?"**

- Binary — enthusiastically yes, or no. Not "probably yes" or "I think so." The hesitation is the signal.
- Full-body check: does your body say yes? Physical/somatic response matters
- Cuts through sunk-cost thinking, tenure bias, and likeability
- Apply during calibration cycles, annual reviews, and whenever a low-level concern has been building for months

If the answer is no or hesitant, you have a decision — the question just surfaces it clearly. Sitting on a "no" and not acting is a leadership failure that compounds over time.

### Living in Your Gifts — Burnout Prevention

The framework: **aim for 80% of your time in activities that use your natural strengths**. At 80%, you're energized and effective. Below 60%, burnout is coming.

**The two-week energy audit**:
- Each evening for two weeks, write down five things that gave you energy and five things that drained it
- After two weeks, patterns emerge that you couldn't see day-to-day
- Use this data to make concrete role adjustments: delegate the draining work, add more of the energizing work

**The mirror question** — ask 5-10 people who know you well: *"When I walk in the room, what shows up?"* Designed to surface your impact on others, your blind spots, and the gap between your self-perception and your actual presence.

Critical reframe: **"It's no one else's job to help you live in your gifts."** Most people wait for their manager to design the right role for them. That's backwards. The manager's job is performance in the hired role — career navigation is yours.

### Co-Founder Dynamics

65% of startups fail from co-founder conflict, not market or product issues. The conflict usually comes from role ambiguity and uncommunicated expectations, not personality.

- **Structural tension**: CTO (skeptic, depth-oriented, detail-focused) vs. CEO (visionary, optimist, big-picture-focused) have opposite cognitive styles. The conflict is feature, not bug — but needs to be named and managed.
- **Enneagram** for self-awareness: understanding each other's core motivations, fears, and stress behaviors
- **"Co-founder vows"**: Create explicit agreements about how you'll handle conflict when it arises — before you need them. Like a prenup. First Round Capital has a useful check-in article for this.
- **"End it or mend it" inflection point**: If you've had the same conflict repeatedly without resolution, you're at a choice. Continuing to repeat it is a decision to let it fester. Either explicitly commit to change or acknowledge the partnership isn't working.

### One-Page Plan (Alpine Investors — People First Operating Rhythm)

A tool from Alpine Investors, linked to documented higher portfolio returns. The structure:
- **Vision / Values**: What we're building and how
- **Strategic Intentions / KPIs**: The directional bets and how we measure them
- **Annual Goals**: What we'll accomplish this year
- **Quarterly Goals**: What we'll accomplish this quarter

Designed to fit on one page. The constraint is the point — if it doesn't fit, your strategy isn't clear enough. Used in team alignment conversations, board updates, and org-wide planning.

### Conscious Leadership

From the Conscious Leadership Group. The key axis: **100% responsibility vs. victim/blame/hero triad**.

- **Below the line**: Victim (it's happening to me), Villain (blame others), Hero (I'll rescue everyone) — each is a form of avoiding accountability
- **Above the line**: 100% responsibility. Not 100% fault — responsibility for your response and your contribution to the dynamic.

Jerry Colonna (executive coach, "Reboot") question: *"How are you complicit in creating the conditions you claim you don't want?"*

This is a powerful reframe for any persistent problem in a team or relationship. If you find yourself in the same bad dynamic repeatedly, you're a variable in the equation.

### AI in Coaching

Rachel uses **Granola** (AI meeting notes tool) + a per-client folder to:
- Capture session notes automatically
- Analyze patterns across sessions ("what themes keep coming up for this client?")
- Build AI-assisted coaching support between sessions by feeding in: session notes + client development plan + relevant frameworks

The implication: AI extends coaching capacity between sessions — the client can ask questions, reflect with the AI, surface their own patterns. High-leverage if the AI has enough context about the person.

## PM Relevance

1. **Level 3 listening in 1:1s**: Most PMs think they're good listeners but operate at Level 2. Level 3 — attending to what's beneath the words — is what surfaces the real problem before it becomes an escalation. Practice: in your next 1:1, notice when you start preparing your response while the person is still talking. That's Level 1 creeping in.

2. **GROW for developing PM judgment**: A Senior Director managing multiple teams should almost never solve problems in 1:1s. Use GROW to build your PMs' judgment — they own the solution, execute with more commitment, and develop faster. Reserve directing for genuine blockers or time-critical decisions.

3. **NVC for stakeholder conflict**: "Stay on your side of the net" is directly applicable when a cross-functional disagreement escalates. The moment you characterize the engineering lead's behavior ("they're being territorial") you've lost the conversation. Lead with observations and needs instead.

4. **Enthusiastic rehire during calibration**: Apply this question to every direct report at your next calibration cycle. The hesitation tells you more than the answer. If you've been tolerating a 70% hire, you're accumulating a team performance tax that compounds.

5. **Energy audit for your own role design**: As a Senior Director you have significant latitude to shape your role. Run the two-week audit on yourself. Which parts of the job energize you (product strategy, customer exposure, mentoring)? Which drain? Systematically delegate the draining work — it's likely someone on your team wants it.

6. **"How are you complicit?" for team dysfunction**: If there's a persistent team dynamic you don't like — slow decisions, lack of initiative, constant escalations — Colonna's question is the most useful one. What in your behavior is reinforcing the pattern? PMs who lead large orgs often inadvertently create the bottlenecks they complain about.

7. **One-Page Plan for roadmap alignment**: Useful for cross-functional alignment at Microsoft scale — engineering, design, data, marketing, legal, finance all have opinions. A one-pager that captures vision, strategic intentions, and quarterly goals creates a single object to react to instead of endless verbal alignment.

8. **AI-assisted coaching model for team development**: Rachel's Granola + client folder pattern is directly replicable. For PMs you develop intensively, maintain a running AI-accessible context file: their development goals, recurring themes, agreed next actions. AI can then help you prep for each 1:1 by surfacing patterns across sessions.
