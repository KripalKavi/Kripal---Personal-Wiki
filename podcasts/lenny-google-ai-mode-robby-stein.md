## Summary

Robby Stein, VP of Product for Google Search, explains how Google built and shipped AI Mode in under a year — one of the most significant changes to the search product in its history. The episode covers Google's product philosophy on AI integration, what enabled rapid execution on a billion-user surface, and why the fear that AI would cannibalize search proved wrong.

---

## Key Takeaways

### AI expands search — it doesn't replace it
- The dominant narrative in 2023–2024: ChatGPT and AI chatbots would kill Google Search
- Google's finding from building AI Overviews and AI Mode: AI features expand the product's utility rather than cannibalizing existing usage
- Users ask more questions, go deeper, and use search for more complex tasks when AI is present
- Strategic implication: the "AI vs. existing product" framing is usually wrong — the right question is how AI expands what users can accomplish

### One-year development cycle for a billion-user product
- AI Mode went from concept to launch in approximately one year
- This is a remarkable velocity benchmark for a product used by billions of people daily
- The enabling factors aren't fully detailed (paywalled), but Stein discusses three core product principles that made it possible

### "Relentless improvement" as a product philosophy
- A philosophy of continuous iteration and refinement over extended timeframes
- The implication: transformative products often look underwhelming at launch and improve into greatness — don't judge new AI features by their initial state

### Persistence is what separates transformative products from abandoned ones
- Most teams abandon potentially game-changing products too early — before they've had time to compound
- Stein's framing: sustained commitment to a product direction, even through early underperformance, is what separates winners
- The temptation in large companies: kill things that don't show immediate metrics gains; this destroys the transformative products

### Stein's product building track record
- Previously built Instagram Stories at Instagram — the response to Snapchat that reversed Instagram's trajectory
- The pattern: he has a track record of building high-stakes, fast-response products at platform scale
- Instagram Stories involved the same dynamics: rapid development on a massive user base in response to competitive pressure

### Jobs to Be Done applied at search scale
- Stein references Clayton Christensen's Jobs to Be Done as a core framework
- Applied to search: the job isn't "find a link" — it's "answer my question and help me accomplish my goal"
- AI Mode expands what search can do for that underlying job without changing the job itself

## PM Relevance

1. **The most direct answer to the earlier query** ("how did Google build AI mode in a year"): Stein's account credits rapid execution to three product principles (paywalled), a "relentless improvement" philosophy that de-risks launching imperfect AI features, and deep persistence on the direction even when early signals were mixed.

2. **"AI expands, not replaces" as a frame for Copilot**: Microsoft faces the same internal debate — does Copilot cannibalize usage of Word/Excel/Teams, or expand it? Google's finding suggests the right bet is expansion. Product strategy and success metrics should be designed around this thesis, not cannibalization defense.

3. **One-year benchmark for velocity**: Google shipped AI Mode — on Search, their most sacred product — in 12 months. This is the competitive benchmark Microsoft is measured against. If Copilot feature cycles are 18–24 months, that's a structural disadvantage.

4. **Persistence as a large-company PM skill**: Large companies kill promising AI features too early because they don't hit short-term targets. Stein's framing gives PMs language for making the case to leadership to sustain commitment to a direction through early underperformance.

5. **Stein's Instagram Stories parallel**: The Instagram Stories story is instructive — rapid platform-scale response to competitive pressure, built and shipped under pressure. That template applies directly to Microsoft's response to competitive AI moves.
