## Summary

Graham revisits her famous “give away your Legos” advice for an AI era. Delegating to a human creates space because ownership genuinely transfers; delegating to AI often leaves the original worker responsible for judgment, quality, and exceptions. The transition therefore creates a double challenge: organizations gain leverage while individuals experience grief, loneliness, and a less satisfying shape of work.

## Key Takeaways

- **The original Lego rule needs an AI caveat.** Keep the work that depends on your distinctive judgment, taste, quality bar, or vision. AI can amplify those capabilities, but is not yet a substitute for owning them.
- **AI delegation is management, not disappearance.** Every worker becomes a manager of junior, non-deterministic digital workers: setting direction, reviewing output, correcting errors, and carrying accountability.
- **Productivity can rise while work gets worse.** More output, more code, or more automation can coexist with more rework, oversight, loneliness, and burnout.
- **Name the grief.** Engineers and product leaders may genuinely miss hands-on building and human collaboration. Treating that loss as irrational blocks adaptation; acknowledging it creates room for reinvention.
- **The durable career advantage is learning velocity.** In fast-changing environments, what someone can learn by tomorrow matters more than what they know today.
- **Replace the fear narrative with agency.** People adapt more effectively when leaders make room to design the future of the profession instead of asking them to protect the past.
- **The “human sandwich” is a useful operating model:** human vision and intent → AI execution and exploration → human judgment, quality control, and accountability.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Give Away Your Legos — with a human/AI distinction

Give away responsibilities as the organization grows so you can take on higher-leverage work. With AI, the transfer is incomplete: execution may move, but context, quality judgment, and accountability remain with the human.

### Human Sandwich

Keep humans at the beginning and end of the workflow: people define the goal and evaluate the result; AI handles intermediate generation, exploration, and throughput.

### Grief Before Reinvention

When a beloved form of work disappears, acknowledge and mourn the loss before demanding excitement about the future. Both grief and opportunity can be true simultaneously.

## PM Relevance

For a Senior Director of PM at Microsoft, the episode argues for measuring AI transformation at the level of **work quality and human experience**, not just hours saved. Design Copilots and agents around intent capture, reviewability, provenance, undo, and explicit quality criteria. Preserve human ownership of product vision, tradeoffs, customer empathy, and the definition of “good.”

Organizationally, do not interpret fewer people per team as pure efficiency. Smaller human teams may lose the collaboration, apprenticeship, and joy that sustain quality. Build new rituals for human connection, give teams permission to grieve obsolete workflows, and reward people who redesign roles around higher-value judgment. A practical leadership prompt from this episode: **“What part of this work should AI amplify, and what part would be a mistake to outsource?”**
