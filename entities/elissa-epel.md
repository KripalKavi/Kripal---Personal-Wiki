# Elissa Epel

## Role / Background

Professor of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences at UCSF whose research examines stress, health, aging, metabolism, emotion, caregiving, and resilience.

## Key Ideas

- Stress can be adaptive when brief, manageable, and followed by recovery; chronic rumination and vigilance are the costly extensions.
- Interpretation shifts physiology: threat and challenge responses are not identical.
- Radical acceptance and “drop the rope” are practices for uncontrollable stressors, not excuses for passivity.
- Resilience requires both effort and release: agency where it matters, receptivity where control is unavailable.
- Coherent narrative and purpose help people metabolize difficult experience and orient toward a meaningful future.

## Relevance

Epel’s work gives leadership a useful calibration layer: the goal is not to eliminate stress, but to distinguish productive challenge from chronic, attention-consuming threat and to build recovery into the operating system.
