## Role / Background

Co-founder and CEO of **[Every](../entities/every.md)** — a Brooklyn-based media + software company (~30 people) that publishes essays on tech, business, and AI's effect on knowledge work, alongside a portfolio of AI tools (Spiral, Proof, Cora, Lex, Quora, hosted OpenClaw). Writes the *Chain of Thought* column. Every runs as an open laboratory of AI-native work — every employee uses Codex / Claude Code / Cowork day-to-day, including non-engineers.

A year ago on Lenny's, Shipper made the off-hand call that *"people were sleeping on Claude Code"* — particularly for non-engineering uses. That prediction validated convincingly enough that he was invited back specifically to make a fresh round of predictions for May 2027.

## Key Ideas (with verbatim framings)

- **"Automation is a lie."** *"Every time you automate something, in order to make sure the automation is working well you need a human on top of it making sure that it's working well."* See [Automation Is a Lie](../concepts/automation-is-a-lie.md).

- **The model mechanism.** *"What models do in general is they make yesterday's human competence cheap. And so it becomes commoditized. It's not valuable anymore. What humans do is we go in there and we're like, 'Yeah, we have all this frozen human competence from yesterday. How do I use this to make something new and interesting?'"* This is the load-bearing claim — automation-is-a-lie follows from it, ride-the-model follows from it.

- **Ride the model.** *"The only thing you need to do is ride the models."* Try each new release on whatever you care about. The edge of AI is wherever AI meets a real human doing something — not San Francisco. See [Ride the Model](../concepts/ride-the-model.md).

- **The reach test.** *"When you wake up in the morning, do you reach for it organically?"* Shipper's internal adoption diagnostic at Every. See [Reach Test](../concepts/reach-test.md).

- **Super-agent over personal agents (a public flip).** Shipper was earlier publicly bullish on OpenClaw-style personal agents and "completely flipped" — companies converge on one super-agent in Slack because every agent needs a human who cares about it, and personal agents don't amortize that cost. See [Super-Agent](../concepts/super-agent.md).

- **CLIs are over.** *"We speed ran the CLI era. It was nice while it lasted."* The future workspace is Codex / Cowork with an in-app browser — agent first, browser second, not the other way around.

- **SaaS thrives, buy the stocks.** Users bringing their own AI tokens *improves* SaaS margins; vendor stops paying for inference, keeps the workflow value. *"What agents do is increase the number of users of SaaS, not get rid of it."* See [Bring Your Own Tokens](../concepts/bring-your-own-tokens.md).

- **PMs and full-stack designers thrive.** "PM Designer thriving." Marcus running Spiral at Every is the named exemplar — lightly technical, spiky product sense, ships faster than nearly anyone.

- **Forward-deployed engineer is the most essential new hire.** Nitesh at Every runs Claudy (the company-wide consulting super-agent) and is the canonical role example. See [Forward-Deployed Engineer](../concepts/forward-deployed-engineer.md).

- **Allocation economy** (essay reference, ~2024). The way humans work with AI is like being a manager — *"and managers actually spend a lot of time working. Most managers are not on the beach."*

- **Senior engineer benchmark.** Shipper built a personal benchmark: vibe-coded codebase + senior-engineer rewrites as ground truth + a prompt that doesn't give away the answer. All models scored 30/100 until GPT 5.5 hit 62; humans score 80s-90s. *"GPT 5.5 is the only model though that has the sense of agency and confidence to just like rip out old code and just actually rewrite from first principles."*

- **Life motto.** *"Do things worth writing about and write things worth reading."* College-era. Pairs it with Rob Burbea's *"relate to it from a position of spaciousness and strength"* for dealing with AI-career anxiety.

## Relevance

Shipper is the single most useful tracking signal in agentic-AI product strategy through 2026 for three reasons:

1. **Public empirical experimenter.** Every is a real 30-person company running every agent product at the frontier. His predictions are reports, not forecasts.
2. **Falsifiable specificity.** He commits to scorecard predictions with a one-year review date. Most AI commentary is non-falsifiable; his isn't.
3. **Willingness to flip publicly.** The personal-agent → super-agent flip in this episode is the kind of update most senior people don't make in public. His arguments compound credibility rather than just accumulating positions.

For Microsoft specifically, his picks (Codex over Claude Code right now, Slack-based super-agent, FDE as the key role, BYOT improves margins) cut against several current Microsoft narratives and are worth scoring against in May 2027.
