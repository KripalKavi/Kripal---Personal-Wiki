# Max Schoening

## Role / Background

Head of Product at **Notion** (2026). One of the rare designer-engineer-PM polymaths in Silicon Valley product leadership.

Career arc:
- Product manager at Google
- Design lead at Heroku
- Design lead + engineer at GitHub, under Nat Friedman
- Two-time founder (one of which was a 2014 Notion competitor — see Failure section)
- Currently Head of Product, Notion

Lives at max.dev. German. Has worked at the engineer / designer / PM tri-section long before "the Venn diagrams started collapsing" — Lenny opened the episode by calling him "made for such a time as this."

## Key Ideas

- **[Cultivating Agency](../concepts/cultivating-agency.md)** — the durable AI-era asset is agency, not skills. Cultivated by *making things*, not by gaming the org chart. The Steve Jobs through-line: *"One day you wake up and you realize the world is made up by people no smarter than you."*
- **"Drive Notion like it's stolen"** — personal mantra he installs in his team. Behave like a founder even when you aren't.
- **[Tiny Core](../concepts/tiny-core.md)** — great products win through one exceptional core mechanic: iPhone multitouch, GitHub pull requests, Notion blocks, Heroku `git push heroku master`, Dropbox menu bar icon, Figma real-time collaboration, Snapchat disappearing photos. *"If I just add one more thing, it'll be finally great. That never works."*
- **[First 10% Is Free](../concepts/first-ten-percent-free.md)** — AI inverts project economics; the bottleneck moves to judgment and shipping.
- **[Malleable Software](../concepts/malleable-software.md)** — long-held belief, now mainstream. Software should serve user interest, not vendor interest. Works with Geoffrey Litt (Ink & Switch).
- **"Demos not memos"** (from GitHub) — react-to-something culture over react-to-PRD culture.
- **[Obviously Good](../concepts/obviously-good.md)** — Notion-internal sticker; only ship things that are obviously good. iPhone and ChatGPT cleared this bar at launch.
- **[Naked Robotic Core](../concepts/naked-robotic-core.md)** — after divergent exploration, do the hard work of consolidation. *"What's actually the core simple thing that should outlive the other evolutionary branches of that same idea?"*
- **[Small Group Theory](../concepts/small-group-theory.md)** — *"The world is run by group chats of eight people or fewer."* Exclusivity is sometimes the right product strategy.
- **Designers should code (but not necessarily ship to prod)** — for the same reason a hardware designer 3D-prints: to interrogate the material. The medium of AI products is the agent loop.
- **Coding harnesses ≈ operating systems of the '90s** — direct evidence for [Harness as Moat](../concepts/harness-as-moat.md).
- **Intelligence retina display** — for most knowledge work, model intelligence saturates; speed and modality matter more than raw IQ past a point.
- **You have to be right, not first** — AirPods, Anthropic, MP3 players.
- **"We already have UBI — it's called knowledge work"** — half-joke; humans will invent reasons to stay in the loop.
- **Counsel to younger SV people**: don't take "the last train." Work hard 18–25, then less. Don't let frenzy distract from what you care about.

## Style as a Product Leader

- Lives in CLIs; thinks in terms of substrate.
- Uses physical metaphors aggressively (3D-printed prototype vs. factory-yield manufacturing) to clarify software stages.
- Reflexively skeptical of buzz: dislikes vibe-coding-as-bragging, token-spend leaderboards, "AI native" labeling, lines-of-code metrics.
- Believes in durability over virality: *"Think of how would you build Ikea — a generational company that is not concerning itself with whatever is trending on Twitter today."*

## Notable Failure (he volunteered)

In 2014 he started what was effectively a Notion competitor. The week they were going to get a term sheet from True Ventures, Notion pivoted from website-builder to document-collaboration and TV had to back out. His team spent enormous effort polishing the editing experience (markdown folding, what's now in Obsidian). Notion's first editor *"was terrible — you couldn't even select between two blocks, but it turns out it didn't matter."* Because blocks were the tiny core.

The lesson he carries forward: *"working diligently on the wrong thing for way too long, huge fail."*

## Relevance

A rare voice that holds three simultaneous positions almost no one else holds:
1. Deep technical fluency (designs in code, lives in the terminal)
2. Senior product leadership at a frontier AI product company (Notion)
3. Skepticism of dominant AI discourse (vibe coding, token leaderboards, SaaSpocalypse, AGI-pilled product design)

Useful as a corrective lens against both the "AI changes everything" frenzy *and* the "AI changes nothing" complacency. His positions on agency, tiny cores, malleable software, and obviously-good shipping are directly applicable to running a large PM organization. Track his Notion product moves as case studies in connected-workspace-as-substrate.
