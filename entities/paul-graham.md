# Paul Graham

**Role**: Co-founder, Y Combinator; essayist; early-stage investor
**Known for**: Y Combinator (world's most influential startup accelerator), essays on startups, design, and intellectual life; Hacker News; early web software (Viaweb)

---

## Background

Co-founded Y Combinator in 2005 with Jessica Livingston, Trevor Blackwell, and Robert Morris. YC has funded companies including Airbnb, Dropbox, Stripe, Reddit, and thousands of others. Graham stepped back from YC's day-to-day operations around 2014 but continued writing essays. His writing draws on his experience evaluating 2,000+ founding teams and his background as a programmer and painter.

---

## Key Ideas

- **Stay upwind**: Optimize for optionality and future flexibility over predetermined paths
- **Curiosity > credentials**: Genuine interest in hard problems outperforms status-seeking; individual talent dwarfs institutional pedigree
- **Good design is redesign**: First versions are prototypes; excellence requires planned iteration, not just shipping
- **Individual > institution**: Variation between individuals far exceeds variation between institutions — applies to schools, companies, and teams
- **Environment as multiplier**: Who you work with and what you work on together dramatically shapes what you can produce
- **Prestige is a lagging indicator**: By the time something is prestigious, the interesting work has usually already moved elsewhere
- **Guard your top idea**: What occupies your idle mind determines what you solve — attention management is a first-order tool

---

## Why Follow

Graham's frameworks are among the most durable in technology and product thinking. They age well because they're grounded in first principles rather than market trends. His writing on design, curiosity, and decision-making translates directly to product management, team building, and strategic prioritization at any scale — not just startups.
