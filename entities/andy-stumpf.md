# Andy Stumpf

## Role / Background

Retired Navy SEAL, former Red Bull high-performance athlete, world-record wingsuit BASE jumper, martial artist, podcast host, and author of *Drownproof*. His wiki relevance is the translation of extreme-performance experience into modest, repeatable practices for agency, discipline, resilience, and honest engagement with pain.

## Key Ideas

- **[Micro-Discipline](../concepts/micro-discipline.md):** Choose the slightly harder useful action more often, particularly in small moments nobody observes.
- **[Influence vs. Concern](../concepts/influence-vs-concern.md):** Put mental load on paper, distinguish uncontrollable concern from influence, and act on the latter.
- **Shortcuts defer and compound work:** Incomplete loops usually take longer to recover than doing the task correctly at first contact.
- **Process confidence:** Long practice can replace the need to predict or control the next opportunity with confidence that one can respond to it.
- **Disclose pain:** Silence preserves the appearance of toughness while removing access to help.
- **Price success honestly:** Maximal achievement can be a bad trade if it consumes relationships, experiences, and fulfillment.
- **High-performance image risk:** An admired external identity can increase isolation when private self-assessment sharply diverges from reputation.

## Relevance

Stumpf offers unusually operational leadership practices without presenting discipline as theatrical hardship. His work is especially relevant to Microsoft leaders managing high-performing teams: close small loops, focus action within influence, price ambitious pursuits, and create conditions where respected people can disclose strain before their competence becomes a cage.
