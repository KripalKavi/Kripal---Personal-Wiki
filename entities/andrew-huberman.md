# Andrew Huberman

## Role / Background

Neuroscientist and host of Huberman Lab, a long-form podcast translating neuroscience and health research into practical protocols. In the wiki, he primarily appears as an interviewer and synthesizer connecting behavioral practices to mechanisms of attention, stress, neuroplasticity, and performance.

## Key Ideas

- Practical tools become more usable when linked to a plausible mechanism and a precise cadence.
- Stress can preserve recall while degrading the ability to form novel connections—an important distinction for executive judgment.
- Repeatedly doing an action one would prefer to avoid may train tenacity; Huberman relates this to the anterior midcingulate cortex, while acknowledging that the broader discussion continues to evolve.
- High-performance systems need serious treatment of mental health because accomplishment and reputation do not confer immunity from crisis.
- Performance protocols should follow a risk hierarchy: behavior first, then nutrition, then supplements, with prescription interventions handled clinically.
- Focus is not synonymous with stimulation: activation, target selection, and sustained pursuit are distinct functions, so more alertness can create agitation rather than useful work.

## Relevance

Huberman's best contribution to this wiki is converting performance advice into testable practices with explicit mechanisms. The executive application requires care: distinguish established evidence, plausible interpretation, and guest experience rather than treating every mechanism discussed on a podcast as settled science.
